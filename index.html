Emirates does not hide much about its recruitment day, but it does not tell you everything either. The airline's own careers page describes three parts. First comes an introduction, where recruiters explain the role and what life in Dubai is like. Then there is an assessment: candidates join group activities and take an online test at the venue. The day ends with a final interview, which Emirates describes as a relaxed one-to-one conversation about your motivation and your customer service experience.

At the moment the airline is not running online assessment days, so everyone has to come in person. To apply, you need to be at least 21, fluent in English, at least 160 cm tall and able to reach 212 cm, with at least one year of hospitality or customer service experience.

Candidates who have been through the day add more detail. They describe reach tests, English tests with grammar, vocabulary and reading questions, and group tasks such as finding unusual uses for an everyday object. Their accounts don't always agree on the numbers, but they agree on one thing: the group activities remove more people than any other stage. Successful candidates then move to Dubai for seven and a half weeks of training.

CH 02 · How the final interview works

Every question has a purpose

The final interview is one recruiter and you, usually 20 to 40 minutes according to candidates. It feels like a friendly conversation, but each question checks something specific. Expect follow-ups such as "And what happened next?" or "What would you do differently?". If you know the type of question, you know the shape of the answer.

Five types of questions

Motivation

"Why Emirates?"
Give 2 or 3 clear reasons: the service, the crew, your growth. Never money or free travel.
Grammar: I'd love to… / I'm looking for…

Experience

"Tell me about yourself."
Present → highlight → personal → future. Who you are now, your best moment, one hobby, why Emirates is your next step.
Grammar: I've been… for [years]

Behavioural

"Tell me about a time when…"
A real story in STAR form: Situation, Task, Action, Result. Most time on the Action.
Grammar: Past Simple: I noticed, I offered

Situational

"What would you do if…?"
Safety first → calm, short words → procedure and senior crew → follow-up with the passenger.
Grammar: I would… / First, I'd…

Curveball

"Which celebrity would you like to meet on board?"
There is no right answer. They test if you stay relaxed. Answer + a reason that shows your personality.
Grammar: I'd choose… because…

What the recruiter is listening for

Customer focusDo you enjoy people and stay warm under pressure?
TeamworkDo you support colleagues and solve conflicts calmly?
Safety awarenessDoes safety always come before kindness?
Cultural respectCan you work with 140+ nationalities on board?
Clear EnglishCan a tired passenger understand you the first time?
PresenceSmile, eye contact, posture, a neat look. They see it before you speak.

Golden rules

  1. Answer the question in your first sentence. No long warm-up.
  2. One real example is stronger than five adjectives.
  3. Keep most answers between 45 and 90 seconds.
  4. Speak positively about your current airline. Always.
  5. Finish with a clear final sentence, not "that's all".

If you get stuck

Everybody forgets a word sometimes. Recruiters care how you recover. Pause, use one of these, and continue in a full sentence.

That's a great question. Let me think for a moment. Could you repeat the question, please? Let me give you an example. What I mean is… Sorry, let me say that in a better way.

CH 06 · The STAR method

Every "Tell me about a time…" needs a shape

Half of the final interview is behavioural questions. Recruiters want evidence, not promises. "I always stay calm" is a claim. A short real story is proof.

SSituation. Where and when? One sentence.
TTask. What was your job in that moment?
AAction. What did you do? The longest part.
RResult. What happened? What did you learn?

    CH 09 · Group activities

    The stage that removes the most people

    Recruiters watch how you work with strangers. Dominating the table fails. Staying silent fails too. Speak in the first two minutes, use names, invite the quiet person, watch the time.
